How a season runs

The scientist never sees the true crop traits or the faults hidden in the field. It works from sensors, drone images, harvests and tests.

Method details

Trial plots are scored by how much they would reduce the yield uncertainty that matters for the plan; a saline plot exposes salt tolerance, a late sowing exposes heat at flowering. Calibration is a Bayesian fit of four traits per crop, and only traits a plot actually exposed narrow. The planner maximises the people fed in the worst tenth of simulated years. An irrigation policy is trained by reinforcement learning inside the scientist's own model (about 40,000 imagined seasons in a minute) and used only if its held-out reward beats the fixed rule. During the season an ensemble model runs beside the farm; a CUSUM detector raises an alarm when measurements drift out of its 90% band.

What can I control?

In a live season you choose; in the recorded season you watch, inspect and explore.

Your crop choices
For each of six fields: a crop and its sowing season, fallow, or leave it to the scientist. The planner warns about poor fits before you start.
The scientist
Plans management, runs experiments, investigates alarms and repairs faults. You can follow every decision, its reason and its evidence.
Surprises (live seasons)
Clog a drip line, release pests, salt the groundwater or send a heat wave, without telling the scientist, and see whether its model notices.
The farm and the scenario
Seven scenarios from a baseline season to a compound one, and the farm seed, water quota, energy budget and starting salinity.
The decisions, if you want them
In human decision mode you choose the first test at each alarm and approve every repair; the ledger records your choice next to the scientist's.
Experiments on your own seasons
Rerun a finished season with one change and compare the pair, start the next season on the soil it left, price it with your own costs, or share a read-only report.
The recorded season
Play, pause, jump between events, inspect any field, read the weather and forecasts, and compare the season with four paired reruns. It can't be changed or re-run here.

You pick, it plans

For each of the six fields you can choose a crop and its sowing season, leave the field fallow, or let the scientist decide. Each crop card shows its sowing window, days to harvest, what it covers (calories, protein or vitamin A) and the yield the model expects on every field.

"People fed" is a model metric: the number of people whose yearly calories, protein and vitamin A would all be covered by the harvest, counted by the scarcest of the three. Wheat alone supplies calories and protein but no vitamin A, so on this metric it covers nobody fully. It is not a full diet or a validated real-world impact estimate.

  • Muse Spark diagnosed 14 of 15 hidden faults (the hand-written protocol 15 of 15) with no wrong calls, but it fed 30 fewer people on average (95% interval −59 to −2; ahead in 9 of 25 pairs). The gap is in its crop plans: on one farm its only vitamin-A crop failed on the saltiest field.
  • Frozen holdout, 8 unfamiliar farms including a faulty sensor and a disease no test can identify: both scientists diagnosed 4 of the 6 identifiable faults, escalated the unidentifiable cause to a person and stayed calm when nothing was wrong; neither caught clogs on generously watered sandy fields.
  • No observed diagnosis gain from the image models in the tested clog and pest cases (one run each; removing SAM 3.1, Spark vision or both left 9 of 10 diagnosed): the field test that separates the hypotheses decides, the photo shapes them.
  • A second frozen holdout (8 more farms, criteria written before the runs) found no measured benefit from Muse Spark over the scripted protocol (−15.3 people fed, interval −34 to +6) and no catastrophic season with or without the planning fix that followed the 26-people loss. WorldLab is a transparent testbed for an AI scientist, not evidence that it farms better.

The AI inside, part by part

WorldLab is an agent: it observes the farm, writes a testable hypothesis, runs the experiment that tells explanations apart, decides, acts through bounded tools, and corrects its own model. Meta's models decide and perceive; numerical code does the agronomy and the learning; a hash-chained ledger records every step. The app shows each tool call as it happens.
